# Primary sources and evidence boundaries

**Product:** One-The-Go Detection\
**Version:** 0.1.0\
**Evidence review date:** 2026-10-10, America/Chicago

This catalog links the scientific and official references supporting the specification package. Each entry states its limited purpose. A cited study, component capability, or guidance document does not validate the integrated product. Published study accuracy, laboratory assay limits, vendor prices, and development targets are different kinds of information.

For requirement ownership and unresolved decisions, see [FULL_SPECIFICATION.md](FULL_SPECIFICATION.md). Experimental design and acceptance gates are in [VALIDATION_PLAN.md](VALIDATION_PLAN.md); component choices and observed retail listings are in [HARDWARE_STARTER_GUIDE.md](HARDWARE_STARTER_GUIDE.md).

## 1. Cannabis chemistry, physiology, and performance

| ID | Primary source | Relevance and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| SCI-01 | Swortwood et al. (2017 issue; online 2016), *Cannabinoid disposition in oral fluid after controlled smoked, vaporized, and oral cannabis administration*. [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5357602/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5357602/) | Oral-fluid disposition and analytical-method evidence; hydrolyzed laboratory measurements must not be silently equated with a free-analyte cartridge. |
| SCI-02 | Vandrey et al. (2017), *Pharmacokinetic Profile of Oral Cannabis in Humans: Blood and Oral Fluid Disposition and Relation to Pharmacodynamic Outcomes*. [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28158482/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28158482/) | Links oral-cannabis pharmacokinetics with measured effects; does not establish a universally applicable saliva threshold for current impairment. |
| SCI-03 | Spindle et al. (2021), *Assessment of cognitive and psychomotor impairment, subjective effects, and blood THC concentrations following acute administration of oral and vaporized cannabis*. [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34049452/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34049452/) | Supports evaluating app-based performance measures; its tested assessment does not validate the proposed shortened choice/inhibition task. |
| SCI-04 | Bird et al. (2026), *Making highways and workplaces safer: An interpretable machine learning approach to predicting recent cannabis use and impairment*. [https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42297468/](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42297468/) | Multimodal precedent using blood, psychomotor testing, and handheld pupillometry; its simulator results cannot be transferred to this saliva/phone system. |
| SCI-05 | Kuijpers et al. (2025), *Eye reactions under the influence of drugs of abuse as measured by smartphones: a controlled clinical study in healthy volunteers*. [https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1492246/full](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1492246/full) | Demonstrates a specific smartphone acquisition procedure; supports controlled lighting and dynamic measurements, not unrestricted selfie-based diagnosis. |
| SCI-06 | Richard et al. (2021), *Alterations in Electroencephalography Theta as Candidate Biomarkers of Acute Cannabis Intoxication*. [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8520987/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8520987/) | Includes ECG-derived cardiac measures alongside EEG; does not establish ECG or a particular HRV metric as a standalone intoxication detector. |
| SCI-07 | Cuttler et al. (2026), *Multimodal examination of the acute effects of cannabis on subjective and physiological stress-related outcomes: a randomized placebo-controlled laboratory study*. [https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-026-02533-9](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-026-02533-9) | Controlled wrist-measurement evidence for physiological responses; it is neither ECG validation nor validation of this proposed impairment classifier. |

These sources justify experiments and specific interpretation limits. They do not establish this product's sensitivity, specificity, calibration, false-result rates, task duration, or required number of participants. The project must obtain those results using its chosen configuration and independent endpoint.

## 4. Project choices that are not published findings

The Nano ESP32 choice, proposed application/backend stack, single-check workflow, `choice_inhibit_v0` trial schedule, approximate two-minute task target, model/result lifecycle, data schema, and US$20/month hypothesis are project decisions. These references do not validate those choices. Their acceptance depends on the experiments, ownership, and open gates in the specification package.

Quantitative product claims require evidence from the exact assay, hardware, firmware, phone configuration, task, model, and intended population. Recheck changing vendor listings, software support, standards editions, and regulatory guidance when implementation or release decisions are made.
