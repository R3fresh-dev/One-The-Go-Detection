## Recommendation

**Start with Johns Hopkins and UC San Diego for scientific fit, and Healgen for an existing-test supply inquiry.** MGH is a targeted lead if custom saliva sensing remains essential; BACtrack is useful later for the separate alcohol channel.

These are prospects to approach, not confirmed partners or investors. Priority reflects technical fit and the proposed first ask, not a measured probability of acceptance. No inquiries were sent. Current partnership interest, pricing, capacity and commercial terms are unverified.

The market split matters: a chemical test can establish exposure, while an impairment product needs an independently defined functional outcome. An assay supplier alone cannot supply the missing impairment validation. A useful first partnership should resolve one of those questions at a bounded cost.

## Why these prospects fit—and what is unconfirmed

### 1. Johns Hopkins BPRU

The repo cites Vandrey and colleagues' research connecting blood/oral-fluid cannabinoid disposition with pharmacodynamic outcomes. That overlaps the project's central chemistry-plus-function question. The proposed first deliverable is a protocol/feasibility assessment, not development of the finished device. A jointly developed study or funding proposal would require agreement on responsibilities, approvals and costs.

**Public route:** [BPRU institutional page](https://www.hopkinsmedicine.org/psychiatry/research/bpru). **Evidence:** [2017 oral-cannabis pharmacokinetic study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28158482/), already cited as SCI-02 in the repo. Institutional availability and current personnel were not verified live. Research collaboration is not assumed free.

### 2. UC San Diego CMCR

CMCR is a strong candidate for evaluating independent impairment outcomes. Marcotte and colleagues' driving-performance trial is a relevant literature lead, but driving performance is not automatically equivalent to a general impairment score. Any study extension would require the institution's scientific and regulatory approval.

**Public route:** [CMCR](https://www.cmcr.ucsd.edu/). **Literature lead:** [2022 driving-performance trial](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2021.4037). The site and this citation were not independently retrieved in this session; confirm current investigators, study capacity and relevance before relying on the lead. There is no evidence that CMCR would fund this project.

### 3. Healgen Scientific

**Strongest verified existing-test supplier lead.** FDA records for K252523 confirm an OTC qualitative oral-fluid cotinine/THC screening system; the THC cutoff is 40 ng/mL. Results are preliminary; confirmatory laboratory testing is a separate step. This supports exposure screening, not impairment. The additional THC lay-user study paired users and explicitly states that no one self-tested. Obtain the actual instructions before assuming the desired solo-use workflow is supported.

**Public route:** [manufacturer](https://www.healgen.com/). **Verified primary evidence:** [FDA decision summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/K252523.pdf) and [clearance letter / sponsor summary](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K252523.pdf). Current standalone THC SKU, inventory, price, domestic distribution, private-label and OEM rights remain unverified. A finished-product supply arrangement would deliver a narrower benefit than the intended impairment device.

### 4. MGH / EPOCH researchers

The repo's chemistry review cites Yu and colleagues' rapid oral-fluid THC assay. It is a targeted lead for the chemical-measurement layer, rather than an established consumer detector or impairment model. Request current technology ownership, commercialization status, evidence access and license terms before assuming the published platform can be used.

**Public routes:** [Center for Systems Biology](https://csb.mgh.harvard.edu/) and [Mass General Brigham Innovation](https://innovation.massgeneralbrigham.org/). **Evidence:** [2021 EPOCH assay paper](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.abe2352), cited as reference 35 in the repo chemistry review. Current technology availability, licensing and collaboration terms were not verified live.

### 5. BACtrack

The repo's earlier source review documents an iOS/Android SDK for connected breath-alcohol products. This could support a separate alcohol offering; it does not authorize a new impairment score or modified measurement workflow. An alcohol development branch should not hold up the first cannabis-feasibility milestone.

**Public route:** [BACtrack SDK](https://www.bactrack.com/pages/sdk). Current live access was blocked. Confirm models, actual supported functions, read-only result import, rights, minimum volumes and commercial terms. This is a supplier/integration lead, not a verified funding source.

## How to approach them with limited money

Prepare one page containing the user, intended result, current prototype status, scientific question and requested first deliverable. Be clear that the repo contains synthetic tools and specifications, with no validated assay or impairment model. Ask for a short fit discussion and a written scope before commissioning work.

For the research prospects, explore a small sponsored feasibility study, a joint funding proposal or relevant existing-study collaboration. These options can still require funding; grant eligibility, open calls and awards have not been verified here. For suppliers, ask for the exact labeling and a small-volume quote before discussing a custom instrument.

Establish data rights, background/foreground IP, publication terms, evidence access, costs and stop/go criteria. No candidate is established as willing to invest, donate work or share proprietary evidence.

**Reserve paid-development option:** [DCN Dx](https://dcndx.com/) is a provisional lateral-flow/point-of-care development lead if finished-product sourcing cannot fit the project. Its current capabilities and pricing were not verified live. Consider only a bounded feasibility quote once funding is available.
