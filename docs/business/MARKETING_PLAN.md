# Marketing and customer-development plan

**Prepared:** October 12, 2026, America/Chicago.\
**Owner:** Jacob Tyrawa.\
**Status:** Proposed execution plan. Targets, messages, channels, prices, and budgets below are hypotheses; they are not results, commitments, or spending authorization.

## 1. Direction and current evidence

The selected path is an **integrated device, application, and model research MVP, followed by an eventual consumer pocket device**. The initial research platform joins chemical measurements, one continuous reaction task, physiological inputs, and traceable records so the proposed combination can be evaluated. A consumer product follows evidence for its exact intended use and the applicable release requirements.

The initial marketing objective is to identify a specific consumer problem worth solving and connect with partners who can address assay and research feasibility. It is not to sell the current platform as a validated self-assessment product.

Evidence available as of this plan:

| Area | Current evidence | Commercial question still open |
|---|---|---|
| Founder | Jacob is a software and AI engineer studying AI at the University of Pennsylvania. He reports leading workplace programs that helped save more than $90 million annually. | Which experience and personal technical contributions most directly support building this product? |
| Software | The repository contains mobile application source, native modules, backend/worker, reader firmware, infrastructure, and recorded software verification checkpoints. | Can the intended complete workflow work with qualified physical measurements? |
| Science and device | The specifications describe a research program and gated progression. The repository does not establish a validated saliva assay, a qualified clinical model, or genuine human-study approvals. | Which assay, partner, independent outcome, and final configuration are feasible? |
| Customers | Jacob describes the intended customer as an everyday adult seeking information to make good decisions. He reports no customer interviews, users, pilots, revenue, or lab/OEM commitments so far. | Which specific situation creates recurring demand, and what do people already do about it? |
| Business model | Approximately $20/month is a proposed consumer subscription. | Hardware payment, test allowance, recurring use, costs, and sustainable margins remain unverified. |

## 2. Consumer segment hypotheses

Start discovery with adult consumers, rather than trying to serve everyone at once. For initial interviews, use adults aged 21 or older in places where their cannabis use is lawful. This discovery choice does not set a clinical study population or establish the future product's supported users.

| Candidate segment | Problem to investigate | Evidence that would support prioritizing it |
|---|---|---|
| **First hypothesis: regular adult cannabis consumers who want to understand variation in their response** | Their subjective impression may not give them the information they want about present functional performance. | Recent, repeated examples; an existing workaround; willingness to complete the proposed workflow; and realistic expectations about its limitations. |
| Occasional consumers, including adults who find edible responses unpredictable | They want information about variable experiences but may need it only infrequently. | A strong recurring problem and sufficient expected use to support either a purchase or another viable payment model. |
| Adults who already track cannabis use or related personal patterns | They may value reliable records and comparisons over time. | Evidence that measurement adds value beyond a diary, existing app, or inexpensive task. |

These are candidate groups, not validated market segments. Interview across them first, then concentrate on the segment showing the strongest observed need. If interviewees primarily seek a safe-to-drive decision, record that mismatch: the current specification does not support that claim, and marketing must not imply it.

Researchers, laboratories, and assay/OEM organizations are development-partner audiences in the selected route. They are not assumed to be paying customers. A paid research-platform business would need its own buyer, procurement, and value evidence before being presented as the initial business model.

## 3. Interview before pitching

Jacob should run the first interviews himself. Begin with behavior and recent experiences before describing the device. The purpose is to learn whether the problem exists, rather than collect approval of the idea.

Suggested questions:

1. Tell me about the most recent time you wanted more information about your response to cannabis. What were you trying to understand?
2. What did you do then? What did that cost in time, money, or inconvenience?
3. How often has that happened recently? When is it irrelevant to you?
4. What information would have helped? What would still be uncertain even with a test result?
5. After showing a clearly labeled concept: what seems useful, burdensome, or confusing about one saliva collection, one short task, and physiological measurements?
6. What would you expect to pay, how frequently would you realistically use it, and what would make you stop using it?

Follow up on concrete behavior. Distinguish an existing purchase or workaround from a hypothetical willingness to pay. Ask permission before keeping notes or sending a later update. Discovery does not require anyone to consume cannabis, provide a specimen, perform a clinical assessment, or receive an impairment result.

Keep contact information and any sensitive interview details outside git. Put only de-identified findings, counts, question changes, and decisions into version-controlled business documentation. Do not include employment-confidential material when describing Jacob's experience.

## 4. Development-stage messaging

Proposed short description:

> We are developing a pocket cannabis-testing device and companion app to investigate how chemical measurements and a short performance task can provide useful information about cannabis exposure and present function. We are speaking with adults about the problem and building the research platform needed to evaluate the idea.

For a concept page or demonstration, identify the development stage prominently. Label fictional chemistry and simulated sensor results where they appear. A software demonstration shows the workflow, not assay performance or clinical accuracy. Describe physiological inputs as proposed research inputs and explain that the final feature set depends on evidence of their value.

Use calls to action such as **“Share your experience”** and **“Get development updates.”** Do not present ordering, a current retail launch, a proven impairment score, a clearance countdown, a legal-threshold judgment, or a safe-to-drive result. The eventual separate alcohol channel remains future scope and should not distract from learning about the first cannabis problem.

## 5. Initial channels and funnel

Start with small, permission-based discovery channels before paying to reach a broad audience.

| Channel hypothesis | Proposed action | What to measure |
|---|---|---|
| Jacob's personal and professional network | Invite people with relevant experiences to an interview without using employer customer lists or implying employer endorsement. | Invitations, responses, eligible interviewees, completed interviews, and segment diversity. |
| Relevant adult consumer communities | Seek moderator permission where needed; share a transparent interview invitation or useful educational material. Respect community rules and individual contact preferences. | Qualified interview requests and repeat problem accounts, not impressions alone. |
| Opt-in education and development updates | Publish accurate material about what exposure tests, functional tasks, and subjective experience can each tell users; link to primary sources. | Opt-ins, replies, unsubscribes, and requests for follow-up. |
| Potential consumer-channel partners | Explore whether suitable adult communities or lawful retail organizations would support discovery conversations or eventual distribution. | Conversations, audience fit, terms, and specific follow-up interest. No partnership is presumed. |
| Assay and research networks | Identify organizations with relevant collection, analytical, reference-method, and research capabilities. Prepare a focused feasibility brief. | Relevant meetings, capability fit, written feasibility questions, indicative timelines, and quotations if offered. |

The proposed discovery funnel is:

**Permission-based invitation or educational content → development-stage concept page → optional interview request or separate update opt-in → interview → permitted follow-up → de-identified segment decision.**

Collect only the contact details and broad eligibility/context needed for discovery. Keep interview requests and marketing updates as separate choices. An update-list signup expresses interest; it is not a user, sale, pilot, or clinical-study enrollment. Publish a simple privacy explanation and provide an easy way to stop updates before collecting contacts.

Clinical study recruitment is a separate workflow with its own approved population, materials, consent, data handling, and responsible operators. A marketing waitlist or permission to receive news does not authorize research participation.

The following numbers are **internal planning targets**, not achieved data, industry benchmarks, or evidence of product-market fit.

| Measure | Initial planning target or method | Decision supported |
|---|---|---|
| Completed discovery interviews | 15 by October 29, with the segment recorded | Whether the first consumer definition can become more specific. |

| Partner diligence | Aim for two relevant capability conversations if scheduling permits | Which assay/research dependencies have plausible next steps. A meeting is not a commitment. |
| Funnel conversion | Record counts and denominators for invite → reply → interview and page → opt-in | Which channel reaches relevant people. No conversion target is asserted before observing a baseline. |
| Update-list quality | Record permission, segment, reason for interest, replies, and unsubscribes | Whether ongoing interest concerns the proposed benefit rather than general curiosity. |
| Pricing | Record current spending, expected frequency, hardware expectations, and reactions to clearly hypothetical offers | Whether a subscription fits the need or another payment model deserves investigation. |

Review findings weekly. Advance a segment when concrete behavior, a recurring problem, and realistic product expectations align. Revisit the proposition if the desired benefit requires unsupported claims, the complete check is too burdensome, or interest depends on a price the cost model cannot support. Present contradictory findings alongside positive ones.

## 8. Staged launch, pricing, and retention

**Research-platform stage:** publish development evidence and recruit conversations, not consumer performance claims. Track partner diligence and progress through the scientific and engineering gates. Research-platform use follows its authorized scope; marketing interest does not close those gates.

**Product-readiness stage:** once feasibility and the exact intended use are supported, test proposed hardware pricing and bounded consumable/service options. Around **$20/month remains a hypothesis**. Reader terms, included collections, consumable cost, shipping, calibration, warranty, support, cloud operation, and applicable study/compliance costs must be specified. Do not advertise unlimited testing or an included/subsidized reader before usage and supplier evidence support the economics.

**Eventual limited consumer launch:** select one evidence-supported segment and one or two channels. Start with a small release within the supported intended use after the relevant release requirements are satisfied. Measure completion, failed checks, returns, support burden, actual use frequency, repeat purchase, cancellations, and margin before broadening distribution.

**Retention:** useful, understandable results and a tolerable complete workflow should drive repeat use. Test whether people value history, consumable access, and relevant service enough to return or subscribe. Separate active use from receiving emails. Use optional reminders tied to the actual product workflow; do not invent a need for daily testing to improve retention. Report retention by starting cohort and elapsed time, along with the sample size.

**Paid acquisition:** all campaigns remain proposed. Consider a small channel test only after discovery identifies a segment and the message matches the available offer. Check the selected platform's applicable cannabis, health, and device advertising rules before preparing a campaign. Measure cost per qualified contact at the discovery stage; do not label it customer acquisition cost before there are actual customers. Eventual CAC and payback require acquisition spend, paid-customer counts, contribution margin, and retention data.
